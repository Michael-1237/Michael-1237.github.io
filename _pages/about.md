I’m a Ph.D. candidate in Theoretical and Computational Science at the University of Lethbridge, where I spend my days (and often nights) wrestling with some of the biggest questions in physics: How does time emerge from the quantum universe? Why does entropy always increase? And what does quantum gravity have to say about it all?

My research sits at the crossroads of quantum cosmology, thermodynamics, and gravity. I’m fascinated by how the arrow of time the one-way flow from past to future might arise from the chaotic quantum soup of the early universe. Using tools from Loop Quantum Cosmology, quantum information theory, and a lot of computer simulations, I explore how quantum fluctuations in the fabric of spacetime could lead to the universe we see today one where time has a clear direction, and entropy always grows.

But I’m not just a theorist. I also work as a Research Assistant in the NeuroEngineering Group, where I get to play with quantum computing and real-world tech. My goal? To make wireless communication faster, smarter, and more secure using quantum algorithms. Think: supercharged signal processing for software-defined radios (SDRs), better ways to track signals in noisy environments, and even quantum encryption for ultra-secure messaging. It’s a wild mix of quantum physics, coding, and hardware tinkering—and I love every bit of it.

At heart, I’m driven by two big questions:

How does the quantum universe "decide" to give us time, structure, and order?

How can we harness quantum weirdness to build better technology today?
